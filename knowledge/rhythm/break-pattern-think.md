# Break pattern: Think (About It)

**Sourcing note (why this entry is `draft`, not `sourced`):** sources were
consulted through search excerpts (Wikipedia, whosampled.com,
attackmagazine.com). Unlike the Amen and Funky Drummer, no source gave a
16th-grid, bar-by-bar transcription of the Think break. Every result
described it in general terms (who played it, when it was sampled, its
character) without exact subdivisions. That structural gap means the
note-level content cannot claim `sourced` under this project's tier rules.
The facts under "What's sourced at the note level" are individually cited. The
notation's hit positions beyond the two named anchors (the opening double
snare, the 2-and-4 backbeat) are authored and marked.

## The rule

### What the break is

"Think (About It)" (1972) is credited to Lyn Collins, written and produced
by James Brown. The drum break is performed by John "Jabo" Starks, not
Clyde Stubblefield; the two are often confused because both drummers are
tied to James Brown-family breaks (Wikipedia, "Think break"; Wikipedia,
"Jabo Starks"). It surfaced on "Ultimate Breaks and Beats Volume 16" (1987)
and spread once it backed Rob Base & DJ E-Z Rock's "It Takes Two" (1988).
With over 4,000 credited samples it is, per Attack Magazine, the
second-most-sampled break after the Amen (Attack Magazine, "The 2nd Most
Sampled Track In History"; WhoSampled, "A Brief History of 'The Think
Break'").

### What's sourced at the note level

Three things:

- **A "double-barrel snare hit"** opens the break: two consecutive 16th
  notes on the snare (WhoSampled synthesis).
- **A backbeat on 2 and 4**, described as a "sharp snare crack" (same
  synthesis).
- **The commonly sampled unit is short**: roughly one bar of 4/4 at around
  113 BPM, shorter than breaks like the Amen (Attack Magazine: "much
  shorter... at 2 seconds"). The 113 BPM figure comes from automated
  tempo-detection sites (Tunebat/SongBPM-style tools), not a drummer or
  producer, so treat it as an estimate.

Nothing else (the hi-hat/ride grid, any kick position, ghost notes, exact
velocities) has a subdivision-level source. Several pages mention "hi-hat
and ride cymbal accents" in general terms, and one mentions a tambourine
layered on top and an unrelated tom fill elsewhere in the recording, but
none place these on a beat grid.
