## The rule

### What the break is

"Think (About It)" (1972) is credited to Lyn Collins, written and produced
by James Brown; the drum break itself is performed by John "Jabo"
Starks — NOT Clyde Stubblefield, a mix-up worth stating plainly since both
drummers are tied to James Brown-family breaks (Wikipedia, "Think break";
Wikipedia, "Jabo Starks"). It surfaced on "Ultimate Breaks and Beats
Volume 16" (1987) and became widely sampled once used as the backing loop
for Rob Base & DJ E-Z Rock's "It Takes Two" (1988) — over 4,000 credited
samples, making it (per Attack Magazine's framing) the second-most-sampled
break in music history after the Amen break (Attack Magazine, "The 2nd
Most Sampled Track In History"; WhoSampled, "A Brief History of 'The
Think Break'").

### What's actually sourced at the note level

Three things, no more:

- **A "double-barrel snare hit"** opens the break — two consecutive 16th
  notes on the snare (WhoSampled synthesis).
- **A backbeat on 2 and 4**, described as a "sharp snare crack" (same
  synthesis).
- **The commonly-sampled unit is short** — roughly one bar of 4/4 at
  around 113 BPM, distinctly shorter than breaks like Amen (Attack
  Magazine: "much shorter... at 2 seconds"). The 113 BPM figure itself
  comes from automated tempo-detection sites (Tunebat/SongBPM-style
  tools) rather than a drummer/producer source, so treat it as an
  estimate, not a measured fact.

Everything else — the hi-hat/ride grid, any kick position at all, ghost
notes, exact velocities — has NO source this session could find at the
subdivision level. Several pages mention "hi-hat and ride cymbal accents"
supporting the groove in general terms, and one mentions tambourine
layered over top and an unrelated tom-tom fill elsewhere in the same
recording, but none place these on a beat grid.
