# Break pattern: Funky Drummer

**Sourcing note:** sources were consulted through search excerpts
(Wikipedia, articles.roland.com, flypaper.soundfly.com, drumstheword.com,
drumforum.org), as with this topic's other break entries. The two-bar
structure and backbeat displacement are corroborated by two independent
pages (Roland's tutorial breakdown and Soundfly's Flypaper history piece)
in different wording, which counts as real corroboration. The exact kick
lattice is where sources conflict (see "A named disagreement" below), and
this entry leaves the conflict visible.

## The rule

### The two-bar shape

"The main drum part is comprised of a two-bar pattern, with a snare hit on
the two and four, a standard sort of 4/4 rock pattern, but with a simple
variation — the four beat hit in the first measure is delayed by one
eighth note" (Soundfly/Flypaper, "A Compendium of History's Most Famous
Hip-Hop Rhythms"). Read literally, bar 1's beat-4 snare moves to the "+" of
4 while bar 2's stays put: the loop resolves instead of repeating the
displacement. The two-bar block preserves this. A 1-bar loop of the
displaced bar alone would misrepresent the source.

### Hi-hat: one continuous line, two documented opens

The groove is "built upon a challenging single handed 16th note hi-hat
pattern," with "subtle single sixteenth note openings on the 'e' of beats
2 and 4, with the first hi-hat opening played on its own whilst the
second opening is played at the same time as the bass drum on the 'e' of
beat 4" (drumstheword.com-style synthesis). The open hat landing with a
kick on the "e" of beat 4 is one of the few kick positions this entry can
cite directly, so it is marked sourced.

### Ghost notes: present, and the hardest one is named

Stubblefield "played a large role in shaping funk drumming... with great
use of syncopation and ghost notes," and "the hardest ghost note to play
[is] the one on the 'a' of beat 3 because it is immediately followed by
the accented note on beat 4" (drumforum.org-aggregated synthesis). That
ghost sits at 3.75 in both bars. The recording has more ghosts than any
excerpt names individually; the rest are constructed, not cited.

### A named disagreement: does the kick land on beat 1?

Roland's tutorial breakdown describes "the bass drum on the 1, and '&' then
'&' and 'e'", a kick on beat 1. A separately aggregated characterization
says "Clyde doesn't play bass drums on beats 1 and 3, creating a funky
groove with no let up", no kick on beat 1. Both cannot be true of the same
performance. This entry treats "no kick on 1 or 3" as primary: it is the
more specific, rule-shaped claim, and funk pedagogy generally credits this
groove with moving the kick off the obvious downbeats. Because of the
conflict, the beat-1 and beat-2.5 kicks are marked `CONSTRUCTED`; removing
them stays consistent with half the sourcing. The one kick position outside
the argument is the "e" of beat 4 (4.25), sourced through its coincidence
with the open hi-hat.
