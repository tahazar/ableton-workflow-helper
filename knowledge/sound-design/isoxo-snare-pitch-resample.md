# ISOxo snare: bounce-and-resample for variation

## Executable
Workflow (stock Live), applied to the finished layered and processed snare
from `isoxo-snare-layering` / `isoxo-snare-bus-processing`:

1. Once the rack sounds right, **bounce/freeze it to a single WAV** (Live:
   freeze the track, or export the selection as audio).
2. Load that WAV into a fresh **Simpler** and use it as the new source
   sample instead of continuing to tweak the multi-layer rack.
3. **Resample** from there: use Simpler's transpose/warp controls to make
   the hit longer, shorter, or pitched up/down, generating new snare
   variants from the one bounced source instead of re-layering from scratch
   each time.

The source gives no semitone range, length multiplier, or pitch direction.
It gives only the technique (bounce first, then resample the bounce, not
the multi-layer original) and the knobs it turns (length longer/shorter,
pitch up/down). Treat any semitone or ms value seen elsewhere for
"ISOxo-style" snares as community convention, not an artist-sourced
number, unless cited separately.

## The rule
Per soverbsounds.com ("How to Make IsoXO Snare"): after the layered snare
is finalized, bounce it to WAV, then resample that bounce ("making it
longer, shorter, more pitched up or down") to generate variations. The
companion "How to Sound Design like IsoXO 2024" piece frames this as part
of a broader habit of "manipulating the timing and pitch of certain sounds"
for detail and variety, not a one-off snare trick.

For arrangement, this is why an ISOxo-style set can have many near-identical
snare hits (fills, alt hits, roll tails) without re-running the full
3-layer build each time: the variants descend from one bounced parent.
