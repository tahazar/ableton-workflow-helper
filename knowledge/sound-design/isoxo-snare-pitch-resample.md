# ISOxo snare: bounce-and-resample for variation

## Executable
Workflow (stock Live), applied to the finished layered+processed snare from
`isoxo-snare-layering` / `isoxo-snare-bus-processing`:

1. Once the rack sums to something you're happy with, **bounce/freeze it to
   a single WAV** (Live: freeze the track, or export the selection as
   audio).
2. Load that WAV into a fresh **Simpler**, and use it as the new source
   sample rather than continuing to tweak the multi-layer rack.
3. From there, **resample**: use Simpler's transpose/warp controls to make
   the hit longer, shorter, or pitched up/down, generating new snare
   variants from the one bounced source instead of re-layering from scratch
   each time.

Source gives no target semitone range, no specific length multiplier, and
no target pitch (up or down) — only the technique (bounce first, then
resample the bounce, not the multi-layer original) and the three knobs it
turns (length longer/shorter, pitch up/down). Treat any specific semitone
or ms value you see elsewhere for "ISOxo-style" snares as community
convention, not an artist-sourced number, unless cited separately.

## The rule
Per soverbsounds.com ("How to Make IsoXO Snare"): after the layered snare
is finalized, bounce it to WAV, then resample that bounce — "making it
longer, shorter, more pitched up or down" — to generate variations. The
companion "How to Sound Design like IsoXO 2024" piece frames this as part
of a broader habit of "manipulating the timing and pitch of certain sounds"
for detail and variety, rather than a one-off snare-specific trick.

Practical implication for arrangement: this is why an ISOxo-style set can
have many near-identical-but-not-identical snare hits (fills, alt hits,
roll tails) without obviously re-running the full 3-layer build each time —
the variants descend from one bounced parent, not from re-layering.
