# Drop phrase evolution across 16-32 bars

How a call/response pair (see `arrangement/call-response-drop-grammar`)
should change across a full drop instead of looping unchanged for 16-32
bars.

## Executable

```awh-notation
# Illustrative 4-bar excerpt of a 16-bar bar-map, not a transcription.
sig 4/4
# Bars 1-2: state the pair plainly, no variation, so it lands as a hook
1|1   G4   1/2  v110
1|3   G4   1/2  v105
2|1   C1   1/2  v127
2|3   C1   1/4  v118

# Bars 3-4: repeat verbatim once more before touching anything
3|1   G4   1/2  v110
3|3   G4   1/2  v105
4|1   C1   1/2  v127
4|3   C1   1/4  v118
# (bars 5-8 in the full map: vary the call only, keep the response;
#  see "The rule" below. Bars 9-12: add a counter-melody under the
#  response; bars 13-16: full drop-variation pass or handoff to the next
#  section)
```

## The rule

- Trap convention treats the drop as 16-32 bars, commonly written as two
  16-bar halves, or as a base drop plus an optional later "drop variation"
  section, rather than one static loop (edmprod.com, "How To Make Trap Music:
  7 Steps To Hard-Hitting Beats"; ourmusicworld.com, "How to Structure a Trap
  Beat").
- To keep a repeating call/response loop fresh across that span, change one
  side of the pair at a time. For example, vary the call's rhythm or add a
  counter-melody in the second half while the response's low sound stays
  fixed. The fixed side keeps the loop recognizable as the same drop
  (elizabethrecords.net, "The Ultimate Guide to Making Trap Beats";
  cymatics.fm, "EDM Song Structure: Turn Your Loop Into A Song!").
- A full second drop variation (a new arrangement pass over the same
  call/response palette) is the standard device for a later
  chorus-equivalent, so the hook returns at least twice without looping
  unchanged for the whole track (ourmusicworld.com; cymatics.fm).
- Micro-tension devices to keep a repeating phrase alive: a one-bar break,
  or dropping an element (e.g. the kick) at the end of an 8-bar phrase and
  reintroducing it as the ear would start tuning the loop out. The reset cue
  is timed to phrase boundaries, not random (edmprod.com, "The Advanced Guide
  to Tension and Energy in Electronic Music").
