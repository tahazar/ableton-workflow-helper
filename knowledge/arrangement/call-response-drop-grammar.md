# Call-and-response drop grammar

Base grammar for writing a two-sound "conversation" drop instead of one
continuous riff. This is general bass-music/dubstep/trap craft distilled
from production tutorials and forum breakdowns — not attributed to any one
artist (see `arrangement/lyny-drop-structure` for what is/isn't sourced
about LYNY specifically).

## Executable

```awh-notation
# ILLUSTRATIVE — constructed from the cited rules below, not a transcription
# of any specific record.
sig 4/4
# Sound A, "the call" — bright/vocal-register lead, keys off the kick
1|1   G4   1/2   v110
1|2   A4   1/2   v105
1|3   G4   1     v100
# beat 4 of bar 1 left empty: the "question mark" before the answer

# Sound B, "the response" — low growl/stab, keys off the snare's aftermath
2|1   C1   1/2   v127
2|3   C1   1/4   v120
# rest of bar 2 left empty: space before the pair repeats
```

## The rule

- Two contrasting sounds trade phrases instead of layering constantly —
  one "asks," one "answers." This alternation, not simultaneous layering,
  is the defining shape of call-and-response applied to a drop
  (dubstepforum.com thread on dubstep drop construction; edmprod.com,
  "How to Write Better EDM Melodies with Call & Response").
- The contrast that makes a pair legible as two "speakers" is carried by
  register and timbre, not just rhythm: per edmprod.com, octave
  displacement (call up/response down or vice versa) and a swap in
  timbre/intensity (e.g. a hard lead call against a soft response, or the
  reverse) are the two levers that make a call-and-response pair read as
  a pair rather than as two unrelated riffs.
- Canonical bass-drop alignment: the low "response" sound (growl/stab)
  lands ON the kick; the bright "call" (lead/vocal chop) sits on and past
  the snare — call and response key off the drum backbone rather than
  floating independently of it (YouTube, "Why Your Drops SUCK Pt. 1: Call
  and Response").
- Working diagnostic: does the response sound like it is talking back to
  the call, or are the two parts just stacked with no relationship? If
  removing either one leaves the other making just as much sense alone,
  it isn't functioning as call-and-response yet (same source).
- Related classical technique, applied to synths: hocketing — splitting
  one bass idea into short motifs and distributing them between two (or
  more) voices, alternating on- and off-beat — is named directly as the
  mechanism behind dubstep bass "punctuations," and those motifs are
  described as the literal building blocks a drop is assembled from
  (soundbridge.io, "Hocketing and Pointillism in Electronic Music").
