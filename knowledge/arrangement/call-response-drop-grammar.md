# Call-and-response drop grammar

Base grammar for a two-sound "conversation" drop instead of one continuous
riff. General bass-music/dubstep/trap craft distilled from production
tutorials and forum breakdowns, not attributed to any one artist (see
`arrangement/lyny-drop-structure` for what is and isn't sourced about LYNY).

## Executable

```awh-notation
# Illustrative: constructed from the cited rules below, not a transcription
# of any specific record.
sig 4/4
# Sound A, "the call": bright/vocal-register lead, keys off the kick
1|1   G4   1/2   v110
1|2   A4   1/2   v105
1|3   G4   1     v100
# beat 4 of bar 1 left empty: the "question mark" before the answer

# Sound B, "the response": low growl/stab, keys off the snare's aftermath
2|1   C1   1/2   v127
2|3   C1   1/4   v120
# rest of bar 2 left empty: space before the pair repeats
```

## The rule

- Two contrasting sounds trade phrases instead of layering constantly: one
  asks, one answers. This alternation is the defining shape of
  call-and-response in a drop (dubstepforum.com thread on dubstep drop
  construction; edmprod.com, "How to Write Better EDM Melodies with Call &
  Response").
- Register and timbre, more than rhythm, make the pair legible as two
  speakers. Per edmprod.com, the two levers are octave displacement (call up,
  response down, or the reverse) and a swap in timbre/intensity (a hard lead
  call against a soft response, or the reverse). Without them the pair reads
  as two unrelated riffs.
- Canonical bass-drop alignment: the low response (growl/stab) lands on the
  kick; the bright call (lead/vocal chop) sits on and past the snare. Call
  and response key off the drum backbone instead of floating free of it
  (YouTube, "Why Your Drops SUCK Pt. 1: Call and Response").
- Diagnostic: does the response talk back to the call, or are the parts
  stacked with no relationship? If removing either leaves the other making
  as much sense alone, it is not yet call-and-response (same source).
- Related classical technique on synths: hocketing, splitting one bass idea
  into short motifs distributed between two or more voices, alternating on-
  and off-beat. It is named as the mechanism behind dubstep bass
  "punctuations", and those motifs are described as the building blocks a
  drop is assembled from (soundbridge.io, "Hocketing and Pointillism in
  Electronic Music").
