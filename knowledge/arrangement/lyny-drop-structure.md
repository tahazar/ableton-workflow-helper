## Coverage note (read before applying)

## What's documented (attributed)

- Trap-leaning bass producer; stated influences are 90s R&B/hip-hop and trap
  (fuxwithit.com, guest-mix interview, 2020).
- Reviewers describe his arrangement instinct as economical, attributing his
  drops' impact to "clarity of purpose" and to giving "each element just
  enough room to breathe without softening the impact". Restraint and space
  around hits are consistent with, but not proof of, the rest-placement rule
  in `arrangement/call-response-rest-placement` (sweetnsourmagazine.com, on
  "Section").
- His low end is repeatedly described as dominant and deliberately
  under-produced relative to peers ("down-and-dirty"; in an album review, a
  "minimal mixdown"): the bass/sub carries sections instead of being layered
  under extra elements (thissongissick.com, on "Face"; edmidentity.com,
  review of the album "Noise To Dance To").
- One reviewer on that album's bass/lead relationship: "scrappy basslines
  clash with brazen leads". This suggests a call-and-response-shaped
  instinct, but it is a journalist's framing, not a producer-stated
  technique. Do not cite it as confirmation of call-and-response
  (edmidentity.com).
- His described workflow on "Face" (paraphrased by the outlet): the track
  followed an off-the-cuff sound-design session, after which he built
  structure around the resulting unconventional sounds. Sound design came
  before arrangement (thissongissick.com).
- Vocal chops are named as a recurring element across coverage
  (fuxwithit.com; thissongissick.com), consistent with a bright
  vocal-register sound as one half of a contrast pair. No source describes
  how he alternates or spaces it against the bass.
